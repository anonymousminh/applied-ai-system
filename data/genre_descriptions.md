# Genre and Mood Descriptions

## Pop
Pop is built around catchy hooks, polished production, and accessibility.
Energy levels are usually moderate to high (0.6-0.95), tempos cluster around
110-140 BPM, and valence (positivity) is often high. Pop pairs well with
"happy" and "energetic" moods, and is a strong default for upbeat scenarios
like commuting, light workouts, and social gatherings.

## Lofi
Lofi (low-fidelity hip hop and chill beats) is defined by relaxed tempos
(60-90 BPM), low energy (0.2-0.45), warm imperfections like tape hiss, and
high acousticness. It is the canonical match for "calm" and "melancholic"
moods, and is widely used for studying, focus, sleep, and emotional decompression.

## Rock
Rock spans a wide range, but the catalog here favors high-intensity rock with
energy 0.7-0.95 and tempos 115-150 BPM. The "intense" mood maps cleanly to
hard rock; "melancholic" rock typically slows the tempo and lowers valence
while keeping guitar-driven texture. Useful for focus, gaming, and strong
emotional release.

## Electronic
Electronic music in this catalog ranges from melodic dance ("energetic" mood,
energy ~0.85, ~125 BPM) to ambient ("calm" mood, energy ~0.55, ~110 BPM) to
darker industrial-leaning tracks ("intense" mood). Electronic is a strong
choice when a user wants flexibility across moods within one genre.

## Folk
Folk centers on acoustic instruments, vocal storytelling, and high
acousticness (>0.75). Energy is generally low to moderate (0.3-0.55),
tempos are slow (80-100 BPM), and the genre pairs naturally with
"melancholic" and "calm" moods. It also has a "happy" branch for brighter
harmony-driven tracks.

## Jazz
Jazz features improvisation, swung rhythms, and instrumental warmth. Catalog
energy is moderate (0.4-0.6), tempos are mid (85-110 BPM), and acousticness
is high (>0.55). Jazz commonly fits "calm," "melancholic," or "happy" moods
depending on subgenre, and is popular for evenings, dinners, and
contemplative listening.

# Mood Descriptions

## Happy
"Happy" tracks have high valence (>0.65), bright tonality, and uptempo or
mid-tempo grooves. Across genres, happy songs often feel social and
outward-facing.

## Energetic
"Energetic" emphasizes drive over emotional positivity. These tracks
typically have energy >0.8, steady tempos 120-145 BPM, and strong rhythmic
density. Energetic songs do not need to be happy.

## Calm
"Calm" tracks have low energy (<0.55), slower tempos (60-100 BPM), and a
sense of space. They often have high acousticness and are chosen for
focus, recovery, or evening listening.

## Melancholic
"Melancholic" tracks have lower valence (<0.45) and often feel reflective.
They can appear in lofi, folk, jazz, and rock, and are sought out for
emotional processing or introspective moments rather than background music.

## Intense
"Intense" tracks have high energy (>0.85) and either high tempo or heavy
texture. Unlike "energetic," intense tracks often feel weightier or darker
and are used for focus-with-pressure, gaming, or hard physical effort.
